# Money in reduces Spending unless it is Pay, a Transfer or Other Income

Daniel pays shared costs (rent, the credit card) in full and his partner sends her half by e-transfer. Money in used to start as Other Income, which lowers nothing, so Spending held the whole cost and the Savings Rate read as negative in Months where he saved. Money in now starts as an Expense with no Category, like money out: it lowers Spending and waits in the Review Inbox. The new Kind Money Back is for a repayment that belongs to no one Category.

## Considered Options

- **Classify the repayments as Refunds in one Category**: no new Kind, but a single e-transfer covers rent and card purchases together, so whichever Category took it would read too low.
- **Keep Other Income as the starting Kind and add Money Back as an opt-in**: errs towards overstating Spending, which is the safer direction, but money in never reaches the Review Inbox, so nothing prompts the User to classify it. That is how the problem went unnoticed.
- **Count Other Income in the Savings Rate**: fixes the rate, but a tax refund or a gift would then make a Month look cheaper than it was.

## Consequences

Spending is net of Money Back, and so are the Spending Limit and the Savings Rate. Money Back has no Category, so the Categories add up to Spending before Money Back; the Dashboard shows both figures. Unclassified money in errs in the flattering direction: a card payment arriving on the card lowers Spending until it is made a Transfer. The Review Inbox is the guard against that, so it now holds money in as well as money out.
